Locking In Elasticity

Before we move on to Lesson 6 and a whole new topic, one session to lock in PED, total revenue, and PES for good, the meaning behind each one, and lots of practice.

No new ideas today. Just making sure the three tools from the last three lessons are fully solid before we build on top of them.
Meaning, part 1

What PED Actually Tells You

Price elasticity of demand (PED) measures how responsive quantity demanded is to a change in price, from the buyer's side of the market.

PED = %ΔQd ÷ %ΔP
Always negative, or zero, since price and quantity demanded move in opposite directions. In practice we usually talk about its size (ignoring the minus sign) to say how strongly buyers react. A big number means buyers react a lot; a small number means they barely react at all.
Meaning, part 2

What Total Revenue Actually Tells You

Total revenue (TR) is simply the money a firm actually takes in from sales, before any costs are subtracted.

TR = Price × Quantity
PED tells a firm what will happen to TR before it changes its price. Elastic demand: a price cut grows revenue, a price rise shrinks it. Inelastic demand: the opposite. This is the whole reason PED matters to a real business, not just a formula to memorise.
Meaning, part 3

What PES Actually Tells You

Price elasticity of supply (PES) measures the exact same kind of responsiveness, but from the seller's side of the market.

PES = %ΔQs ÷ %ΔP
The one thing that flips: PES is almost always positive, since the law of supply says price and quantity supplied move in the same direction. PED asks "how much do buyers react?" PES asks "how much can sellers react?" Same shape of formula, opposite side of the market, different sign.
Why any of this matters

The Real-World Payoff

These aren't just exam formulas. Each one answers a real question a real decision-maker actually needs answered.

For a firm like Tom's Print Co — should I raise or cut my price to grow revenue? PED (and TR) answers it directly.

For a government — if I tax an inelastic good like cigarettes, will people keep buying it anyway (and the tax mostly just raises revenue), or will demand collapse? PED again.

For a producer deciding how fast to expand — can I actually ramp up supply quickly if price rises, or am I stuck waiting? That's PES.
