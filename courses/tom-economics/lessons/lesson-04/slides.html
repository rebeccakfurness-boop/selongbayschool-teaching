Tom puts up the price of his padel grips. More money per grip sold... but does he end up with more total money?

Guess — and tell me why.
Total revenue
TR = P × Q
A price change moves P. But it also moves Q, because of PED. Those two moves can fight each other — that's the whole lesson.

The rule
PEDPrice risesPrice falls
Inelastic (<1)Revenue risesRevenue falls
Elastic (>1)Revenue fallsRevenue rises
Unitary (=1)No changeNo change
Why it matters
Firms
Use PED to set prices for maximum revenue — raise prices on inelastic products, avoid raising (or cut) prices on elastic ones.
Consumers
Inelastic necessities hurt household budgets most when prices rise — you can't easily cut back.
Government
Taxes work best on inelastic goods (fuel, cigarettes) — quantity barely falls, so tax revenue stays high and reliable.

Recap
TR = P × Q

Inelastic demand: price & revenue move together.
Elastic demand: price & revenue move opposite.

That's why PED matters to firms' pricing, consumers' budgets, and government tax choices.
