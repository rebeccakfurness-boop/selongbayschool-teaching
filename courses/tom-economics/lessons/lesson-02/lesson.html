The Padel Club's Problem

The club has a fixed number of courts. Demand is wildly different at different times of day.

9am weekdays: courts regularly sit empty.

Same price both times. What's going on?
New idea

When Price Gets It "Wrong"

You already know equilibrium: the price where quantity demanded exactly equals quantity supplied. But real markets don't always sit at that exact price.

Whenever the current price is not the equilibrium price, quantity demanded and quantity supplied won't match. Economists call this market disequilibrium. It comes in two opposite flavours — and the padel club actually has both happening on the same day, at different times.
Two flavours

Shortage vs Surplus

Shortage: quantity demanded is greater than quantity supplied at the current price. There isn't enough to go around.
Surplus: quantity supplied is greater than quantity demanded at the current price. There's too much sitting unused.

How markets fix themselves

The Price Mechanism

Disequilibrium doesn't last forever — price does the fixing.

When there's a shortage, sellers notice far more buyers want the product than they can supply. They raise the price. That reduces quantity demanded (a movement along the demand curve) until it matches the fixed quantity supplied — a new, higher equilibrium.
When there's a surplus, sellers notice unsold stock piling up. They cut the price to attract more buyers. That increases quantity demanded until it matches quantity supplied — a new, lower equilibrium.

This is exactly why the padel club could charge more at 6-8pm and less at 9am — it's pushing each time slot's price toward its own true equilibrium.

Watch out

Causes vs. Consequences

This is the easiest thing to mix up in this whole topic — worth being really precise about.

A shortage or surplus is not what causes a price to change — it's the other way round. The actual chain is: something changes (e.g. more people want padel) → that shifts demand and/or supply → that creates a shortage/surplus at the old price → that is what pushes the price to move.

A shortage/surplus is a symptom that price hasn't caught up yet — not the original cause.
