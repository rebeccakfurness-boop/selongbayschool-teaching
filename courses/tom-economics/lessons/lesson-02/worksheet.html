Answer key (tutor copy — not for Tom)

1a. A situation where quantity demanded and quantity supplied are not equal at the current price (the market is not at equilibrium).
1b. Shortage: quantity demanded exceeds quantity supplied at the current price. Surplus: quantity supplied exceeds quantity demanded at the current price.

2a. Shortage — quantity demanded (people wanting the slot) exceeds quantity supplied (fixed number of courts) at the current price.
2b. Surplus — quantity supplied (courts sitting empty) exceeds quantity demanded at the current price.
2c. Raising the evening price reduces quantity demanded (movement along the demand curve) until it matches the fixed quantity supplied — clearing the shortage at a new, higher equilibrium for that slot. Lowering the morning price increases quantity demanded until it matches quantity supplied — clearing the surplus at a new, lower equilibrium for that slot.

3a. A shortage emerges — quantity demanded now exceeds the fixed/shrinking quantity supplied at the old retail price.
3b. The resale price rises, because as price rises, quantity demanded falls back until it matches the fixed quantity available — the market moves to a new, higher equilibrium.

4a. A change in demand and/or a change in supply (a shift of one or both curves), driven by changes in their non-price determinants.
4b. The quantity bought and sold changes (the exact size of the change depends on elasticity — covered next lesson).
4c. False — a price change alone causes a movement along an existing curve, not a shift of the curve itself. A shift is caused by a change in a non-price determinant (income, tastes, cost of production, etc.).
