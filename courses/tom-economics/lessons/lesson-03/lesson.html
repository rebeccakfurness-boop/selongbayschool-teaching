Introducing PED

Price elasticity of demand (PED) measures how responsive quantity demanded is to a change in price — in other words, exactly the question we just asked about Tom's grips.

PED = %ΔQd ÷ %ΔP
Read it as: "the percentage change in quantity demanded, divided by the percentage change in price." It's a ratio — it tells you how many times bigger (or smaller) the reaction in quantity is, compared to the change in price that caused it.

PED is technically always negative (law of demand: price and quantity move opposite ways) — economists usually just discuss its size, ignoring the minus sign.

Naming the Categories

A PED number on its own isn't that useful to say out loud in an exam — Cambridge wants you to know which category it falls into, because each category behaves differently and matters for different reasons (we'll see exactly why next lesson, when we look at revenue). There are five categories, running from "doesn't react at all" to "reacts infinitely."
