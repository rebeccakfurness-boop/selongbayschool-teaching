Lesson 3 · Syllabus 2.6
Price Elasticity of Demand
If Tom raises his prices, does he lose a few customers — or a lot?
Same price rise, different reactions
Tom puts up the price of custom padel grips by 10%. Would he lose a few customers, or a lot? What would that actually depend on?
Definition & formula
PED measures how responsive quantity demanded is to a change in price.
PED = %ΔQd ÷ %ΔP
PED is technically always negative (law of demand) — economists usually just discuss its size, ignoring the minus sign.
🎾 Worked example: elastic
Padel club casual court booking
Price: $20 → $22   (+10%)
Quantity: 100 → 80 bookings/week   (−20%)
PED = −20 ÷ 10 = −2 → Elastic
🧩 Worked example: inelastic
Retired Lego set, resale market
Price: $50 → $55   (+10%)
Quantity: 40 → 38 sets/month   (−5%)
PED = −5 ÷ 10 = −0.5 → Inelastic
The PED spectrum
Perfectly inelastic
PED = 0
Inelastic
0 < PED < 1
Unitary
PED = 1
Elastic
PED > 1
Perfectly elastic
PED = ∞
Determinants of PED (1/2)
Substitutes
More/closer substitutes = more elastic. A generic Lego set (many alternatives) vs. a specific retired set (no substitute).
Necessity vs luxury
Necessities are more inelastic. Court resurfacing (has to happen) vs. novelty keychains (impulse buy).
Share of income
Bigger share of income = more elastic. A cheap grip (barely noticed) vs. an annual club membership (very noticeable).
Determinants of PED (2/2)
Habit-forming
Addictive goods are more inelastic (classic example: cigarettes).
Time period
More elastic in the long run — customers keep buying out of habit short-term, then switch supplier over time.
Width of definition
"3D-printed products" broadly (hard to avoid) vs. "Tom's specific grip design" narrowly (easy to switch away from).
Quick check
💡 A 10% price rise causes quantity demanded to fall by 25%. Elastic or inelastic?

💡 Which makes demand more elastic: many substitutes, or being a necessity?
Recap
PED = %ΔQd ÷ %ΔP

0 → perfectly inelastic  ·  0–1 → inelastic  ·  1 → unitary  ·  >1 → elastic  ·  ∞ → perfectly elastic

Determinants: substitutes, necessity, income share, habit, time, width of definition.
