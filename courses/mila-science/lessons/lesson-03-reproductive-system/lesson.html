To start

Growing Up

You've already grown so much since you were a baby. You're taller, stronger, and can do things you couldn't do before.

Your body isn't finished changing yet. Over the next few years, it will go through another big round of changes, called puberty. Today, let's learn exactly what happens, and why.
New idea

What Is Reproduction?

Before we get to puberty, let's start with the big question: why do living things need to reproduce at all?

Reproduction means making new living things of the same kind, like plants making seeds, or animals having babies. It's part of every living thing's life cycle: born, grow, and (eventually) reproduce, so the cycle can continue in the next generation.

Which stage of that cycle are you in right now?

New idea

Meet Puberty

The stage where your body becomes able to reproduce is called puberty.

Puberty is controlled by chemical messengers in your body called hormones. Hormones tell your body to start growing and changing in new ways.

Puberty doesn't happen on an exact birthday, it starts at different ages for different people. On average, it often starts around age 11 for girls and around age 13 for boys, but plenty of people start earlier or later, and that's completely normal.
First sign

The Growth Spurt

One of the very first signs of puberty is a growth spurt.

Girls can grow up to about 12 cm in a single year. Boys can grow up to about 10 cm in a year. That's a lot faster than the year before!
New idea

Body Changes During Puberty

Hormones cause a whole set of changes, and not everyone experiences exactly the same ones.

Changes many girls notice
Hips get a bit wider, breasts start to develop, body hair grows, skin can get oilier, voice can get a little deeper.
Changes many boys notice
Shoulders and chest get broader, facial and body hair starts growing, voice gets noticeably deeper, skin can get oilier.

Some changes, like more body hair and oilier skin, happen to pretty much everyone.

New idea

What Actually Changes Inside?

So what's really going on inside your body during puberty?

The reproductive system's one job is to make special cells called sex cells, the cells needed to make a new baby. Puberty is when your reproductive system switches on and starts doing this job for the very first time.
New idea

Meet the Sex Cells

Sperm
The male sex cell. A boy's body starts making sperm during puberty.
Ova (eggs)
The female sex cell. One egg is called an ovum. A girl's body starts developing ova during puberty.

New idea

Fertilisation

When a sperm and an egg (ovum) join together, that's called fertilisation. It usually happens inside the oviduct.

Once fertilisation happens, the fertilised egg can grow into a baby, developing inside the uterus for about nine months.

New idea

Menstruation

One more thing that starts happening in girls, usually about a year after puberty begins.

About once a month, if an egg released by the ovaries isn't fertilised, it leaves the body along with the lining of the uterus. This is called menstruation (sometimes called a period).

It's a completely normal, healthy process, though it's often irregular to start with, while a girl's body is still getting used to it.
