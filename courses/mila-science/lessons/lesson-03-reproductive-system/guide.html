Lesson 3: Comprehensive Teaching Script

Cambridge Primary Science, Stage 6, Unit 1.3 The reproductive system
Linked materials: worksheet.html / worksheet.docx, lesson.html (interactive)

A note before you start: this is a more sensitive topic than the last two lessons, so a few things worth knowing before you begin. The content matches the actual Cambridge Primary Science Learner's Book 6 exactly in scope: puberty changes, correct anatomical names for the reproductive organs, sex cells, fertilisation, and a brief factual mention of menstruation. Nothing here goes beyond that curriculum. The tone throughout is deliberately matter of fact and clinical, the same register the real textbook uses, not embarrassed or overly casual. If she asks a question you don't want to answer on the spot, "that's a great question, let's come back to it" is a completely fine response, you don't need to have every answer ready.

Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson she can:

  1. Explain what puberty is and describe some of the body changes it causes.
  2. Name the main parts of the male and female reproductive systems and what each one does.
  3. Explain what sex cells are, and what fertilisation means.
  4. Describe, in order, the basic sequence from sex cells to a baby being born.

Step-by-step script

Step 1: Last time and today

Say: "Last time was the respiratory system, breathing. Today's a new body system: the reproductive system. It's about how your body grows and changes as you get older, and how new life begins."

Step 2: Growing up

Read the card together. This is deliberately a gentle, normalising opener before any new vocabulary shows up, no need to rush it.

Step 3: What is reproduction?

Ask her: "Why do you think living things need to reproduce at all?" Any sensible answer is fine here, the point is the general idea (continuing a species), not a precise definition.

Step 4: Meet puberty

Say: "Puberty is controlled by hormones, chemicals in your body that trigger these changes." Note the ages given (around 11 for girls, around 13 for boys) are averages, not rules, worth saying out loud that starting earlier or later is completely normal.

Step 5: The growth spurt

Quick read, this is a fun concrete number (10 to 12 cm in a year) that makes the abstract idea of "puberty" feel real.

Step 6: Body changes during puberty

Read both columns together. If she asks about a change not listed, that's a good sign she's engaged, answer honestly and simply.

Step 7: What actually changes inside

This is the bridge from "outside changes" to the new vocabulary coming next: sex cells.

Step 8: Meet the sex cells

Ask her: "Can you guess what the male sex cell is called? The female one?" before revealing the cards, if she's heard either term before this checks prior knowledge gently.

Step 9: The male reproductive system

Three tap cards: testes, sperm duct, penis. Use the correct anatomical names throughout, matter of fact, no euphemisms.

Step 10: The female reproductive system

Four tap cards: ovaries, oviduct, uterus, birth canal.

Step 11: Fertilisation

Say: "This is the key moment, when a sperm and an egg actually join together."

Step 12: The journey to a new life

Read all five steps together, then ask her to recount the sequence in her own words. This step deliberately ties together everything from steps 9 to 11 into one narrative.

Step 13: Menstruation

Keep this factual and brief, exactly as written. If she has follow-up questions, answer plainly; there's no need to add detail beyond what's asked.

Step 14: Quick questions

Six guess-then-reveal cards covering everything so far.

Step 15: Male or female reproductive system?

A sort activity classifying seven organs. This is genuine recall, not a guess, let her work through it herself first.

Step 16: Quiz

Five multiple-choice questions.

Step 17: Vocab recap

Read through all seven key words together.

Step 18: Homework

Set the worksheet for her own time.

Wrap-up

This is a one-lesson introduction, not the only conversation you'll ever have about this topic, treat it that way. If something feels like it needs more time than this single 40-minute session allows, it's completely fine to pause and pick it back up another day rather than rushing to the end.
