Why it matters

Why Does Your Heart Beat?

Your heart is a very special muscle. Its one job is to pump blood all around your body, all day and all night, without ever stopping — even while you sleep!

Every time your heart squeezes to push blood out, that's one heartbeat. It takes less than a minute for your heart to send blood all the way round your whole body and back again.

Think about it: what do you think would happen to the rest of your body if your heart ever stopped, even for a few seconds?

Try this

Feel Your Own Pulse

Every time your heart beats, it pushes blood through your body with a little pulse of pressure — you can actually feel it! Try this now:

Place two fingers gently on the inside of your wrist, or the side of your neck. Stay still for a moment. Can you feel a small beat under your skin? That's your pulse — one beat for every heartbeat.

Try counting how many beats you feel in 15 seconds, then multiply by 4 — that's roughly your heart rate for one minute! Was it faster or slower than you expected?

New idea

What Does Blood Actually Carry?

Your heart isn't just pumping blood around for no reason — blood is a delivery service for your whole body. Why do you think your cells might need things delivered to them?

On the way out: blood carries food and oxygen to every single cell in your body, so they have the fuel and the air they need to work.

On the way back: blood picks up waste your cells don't need any more — like carbon dioxide — and carries it away to be got rid of.

New idea

Meet the Blood Vessels

Blood doesn't just float around loose inside you — it travels through tubes called blood vessels, a bit like water flowing through pipes, or cars driving on roads.

There are three kinds of blood vessels, and each one has a different job:

Arteries — carry blood away from the heart
Veins — carry blood back to the heart
Capillaries — tiny tubes that connect arteries to veins, where blood actually drops off its delivery

Quick guess: right after a heartbeat, blood is pushed out under high pressure. Which do you think needs thicker, stronger walls — the tube right next to that push, or the tube on the quiet journey back?
