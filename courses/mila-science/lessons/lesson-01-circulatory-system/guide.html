Lesson 1: Comprehensive Teaching Script

Cambridge Primary Science, Stage 6 · Unit 1.1 The circulatory system
Linked materials: worksheet.html / worksheet.docx · lesson.html (interactive)

Second rebuild note: the first rebuild treated this as a totally fresh start, which undersold what she'd already been exposed to. This version opens by explicitly naming what was covered last time (organs, a first look at blood vessels) and frames today as going deeper on the heart specifically before circling back to blood vessels with better grounding, not starting over. It also trades several paragraph-style explanation cards for shorter bulleted/two-column visuals, and adds real extra heart content (the heart's two sides, a set of "did you know" facts) that the first rebuild was missing.

Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson she can:

  1. Recall organs and roughly where they're found (recap from last time).
  2. Explain that the heart has two sides: left pumps oxygen-rich blood to the body, right pumps oxygen-poor blood to the lungs.
  3. Explain that blood carries food and oxygen to cells, and carries waste away.
  4. Correctly classify arteries, veins, and capillaries by direction and wall thickness, including the "artery to the lungs" exception.
  5. Describe, in order, the full loop blood travels: heart → lungs → heart → body → heart.

Step-by-step script

Step 1: Last time & today

Say: "Quick recap of where we got to last time, then here's today's plan."
Read both cards together. Say explicitly: "Last time we met the organs, and had a first look at arteries, veins, and capillaries, but honestly, that jumped in a bit fast. Today we're going to properly understand the heart first, then those blood vessels will make a lot more sense when we come back to them."

Step 2: Organs recap

Six tap-cards, quick pace: this is recap, not new content. Don't dwell; if she remembers most of these quickly, move on.

Step 3: Zooming in on the heart

Say: "Hand on your chest. Feel it? That's working right now." Ask her: "Point to exactly where you think your heart is", then move to the diagram.

Step 4: Meet your heart

Compare her guess to the diagram. Ask: "Was that close?"

Step 5: Your heart has two sides (NEW depth)

Say: "Here's something new: your heart isn't one pump, it's really two pumps side by side."
Ask her: "Which side do you think sends blood to your lungs, and which side sends it to the rest of your body?" Let her guess before revealing the two cards. Flag explicitly: "Remember this, it matters again later when we look at the full loop."

Step 6: Why does it never stop?

Read the bulleted facts together. Ask her: "What do you think would happen to the rest of your body if it ever did stop, even for a few seconds?"

Step 7: Amazing heart facts

Four tap-cards of genuinely surprising facts (100,000+ beats a day, a pool of blood pumped daily, beating since before birth, fist-sized). Ask her which one surprised her most, this is deliberately the "fun" beat of the lesson, let it be fun.

Step 8: Feel your own pulse

Do this together, physically, exactly as before: fingers on wrist or neck, count 15 seconds, ×4.

Step 9: What does your heart actually pump?

Ask first: "Why might your cells need things delivered to them?" Then reveal the two-column card: food+oxygen out, waste back.

Step 10: Remember those blood vessels?

Say, explicitly bridging back: "Last time we started looking at arteries, veins and capillaries, without really knowing how the heart worked yet. Now you do, so let's make proper sense of them."

Step 11: Meet the blood vessels

Ask her a real prediction: "Right after a heartbeat, blood gets pushed out hard. Which tube needs thicker walls: right next to that push, or on the calm way back?"

Step 12: Tap cards: arteries, veins, capillaries

Check her Step 11 prediction against what the cards say, and tell her explicitly if she predicted correctly.

Step 13: Sort: artery, vein, or capillary?

Five statements. The last one (an artery to the lungs, despite carrying no-oxygen blood) is deliberately the trickiest: this is precisely the idea her original worksheet expected her to work out alone. Explain if needed: "arteries and veins are named by direction, not by what they're carrying."

Step 14: The journey of blood

Read all six steps together, then have her recite the order back from memory. This version explicitly names left/right side in the steps: tie it back to Step 5.

Step 15: The loop diagram

Ask her: "Point to where blood picks up oxygen. Now where does it drop it off?"

Step 16: Quick questions

Eight guess-then-reveal cards, covering everything, including the left/right side content specifically.

Step 17: Quiz

Five multiple-choice questions, including one on the heart's two sides. If she gets one wrong, ask her to reason it through before correcting.

Step 18: Vocab recap

Read through the six key words together.

Step 19: Homework

Set the worksheet for her own time.

Wrap-up

The biggest structural change from the first rebuild: this one doesn't pretend last week didn't happen. Naming the gap honestly ("that went a bit fast, let's slow down and do it properly") tends to land better with a student than silently restarting, since it validates that the difficulty was real without making her feel like she failed at something.
